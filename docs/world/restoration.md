# Restoration as world design

**Status:** Canonical narrative and restoration principles summarized from the [World Restoration Foundation](https://github.com/Shashakar/RPG-Kingdom/blob/main/docs/WORLD_RESTORATION_FOUNDATION.md).

## Identity anchors

A person's return is tied to something that makes them *them*: a **place**, **possession**, **relationship**, **duty**, or **memory**. Requirements are authored per person; there is no universal restoration currency.

| Anchor | Story question | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Place | Where did they belong? | Home, forge, library, shrine |
| Possession | What object holds meaning? | Hammer, journal, keepsake |
| Relationship | Who connects them to life? | Family, friend, oath |
| Duty | What work defined them? | Craft, protection, service |
| Memory | What knowledge remains missing? | Records, symbols, testimony |

## First-session narrative pattern

**House Wisp → Restore House → Restored resident → Blacksmith Wisp → Restore Forge + Find Hammer → Spectre → recover Hammer through adventure → Restored Blacksmith → settlement route consequence.**

This sequence is the source-recorded first-session direction, not a requirement to repeat the same structure in every settlement.

## World consequence

Restoration should dismantle **specific advantages** of the Soul Dragon: lost wards can return, roads can reopen, specialists can recover, corruption sources can be disabled. Avoid reducing every consequence to a generic global strength percentage.

## The central Keep

The Keep is a persistent home base and mirror of the player's wider accomplishments. Storage, visitors, services, and trophies are possible functions, but it should not become a mandatory management chore. Exact facilities are not all approved for V1.

## End of the initial campaign

The Soul Dragon is intended to provide a complete first-release ending. A later expansion must not immediately invalidate that victory.

## Review questions

- Is each restored person linked to understandable anchors?
- Does the visible change communicate a meaningful social or functional difference?
- Does the player know what remains incomplete?
- Can the player identify a concrete consequence of restoration?
- Does this settlement have its own identity, rather than repeating a generic checklist?
