# Friction, infrastructure and travel

**Status:** Source-recorded long-term design constraints; travel systems are not V1 scope.

> Convenience should often be earned through world improvement. Once earned, it should be genuinely convenient.

## Friction with a purpose

Geography, scarcity, danger, preparation, and isolation can create meaningful decisions and memorable before/after progression. Poor UI, unclear controls, or repeated waiting after the player has solved the underlying problem are not desirable friction.

The player should experience a broken bridge, unsafe road, or missing service *before* its restoration makes travel, trade, or life easier.

## Distinct travel roles

| Mode | Potential world function |
| --- | --- |
| Walking and roads | Broad accessibility, patrols, carts, local trade |
| Caravan / coach / ferry | People and cargo dependent on safe infrastructure |
| Flight | Fast travel for limited people and loads |
| Portal | Highly convenient but potentially disruptive to geography |

Portals must not casually erase the logic of roads, regional scarcity, sieges, caravans, or isolation. Possible limitations—fixed nodes, capacity, cost, operators, recharge, or instability—are **candidates**, not a committed portal specification.

## Player convenience versus physical logistics

An abstracted fast journey for one adventurer need not imply instant movement of armies or bulk supplies. The fiction must still explain why regions remain distinct.

## Design questions for any infrastructure unlock

- What problem did the player experience?
- What exactly changes when the route or service is restored?
- Who and what can use it?
- Does the unlock preserve believable geography and local economies?
- Is the convenience substantial enough to feel earned?
