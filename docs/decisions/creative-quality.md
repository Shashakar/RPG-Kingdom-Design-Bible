# Creative quality and agent-produced content

**Status:** Project conversation-derived quality target; detailed rubric proposed.

## Quality expectation

The creative director has set a target for environment/level composition equivalent to an experienced professional designer, **at least four years of experience**, ideally approaching senior judgment. This is a *quality aspiration*, not a claim that automated scene composition currently achieves it.

A scene is not good merely because it compiles, passes collision checks, or technically satisfies a short issue description. The game must feel intentionally designed at player scale.

## Proposed acceptance rubric

| Dimension | Evidence to examine |
| --- | --- |
| Visual hierarchy | Clear destination, route, landmarks and focal points |
| Material language | Distinct, coherent terrain, path, rock and architecture treatments |
| Environmental life | Appropriate vegetation, dressing and signs of habitation |
| Spatial craft | Varied silhouettes, composition, scale, framing and meaningful negative space |
| Continuity | Terrain and adjacent spaces connect without arbitrary boundaries |
| Gameplay fit | Tutorial, movement, combat, camera, navigation and interaction remain legible |
| Transformation | Damaged/restored states communicate actual progress |

## Creative review protocol

1. Begin with design intent and player task, not just a list of Unity operations.
2. Establish constraints and approved asset sources.
3. Produce a bounded authored composition and capture gameplay-distance evidence.
4. Evaluate visual craft **separately** from technical validation.
5. Record changes requested, accepted, or still unresolved.
6. Treat the human creative decision as the final sign-off.

## Authority boundary

The Design Bible can define desired visual outcomes, but it does not grant broad access to production scenes. Follow the [game's scene authoring policy](https://github.com/Shashakar/RPG-Kingdom/blob/main/docs/SCENE_AUTHORING.md) for issue-specific permissions.
