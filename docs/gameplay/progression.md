# Character growth, classes and professions

**Status:** Source-recorded **future design direction**, not authorization for V1 implementation. The authoritative progression document supersedes older assumptions.

## Four concepts

- **Competency:** capability developed through meaningful practice.
- **Knowledge:** techniques and understanding acquired through the world, teaching, and discovery.
- **Mastery:** deeper achievement beyond routine competence.
- **Dedication:** a path or class commitment that changes which advanced possibilities are available.

## Classless beginnings

Characters should begin without a formal class and explore foundational capabilities. Later class dedication represents substantial commitment, not a level-based bundle of unrelated skills. Greater depth should create greater specialization and opportunity cost.

Hybrid paths may arise when prerequisite paths genuinely intersect—for example, martial practice plus relevant arcane knowledge—rather than arbitrary stacks of class levels. The exact classes, thresholds, and hybrid names remain undecided.

## Meaningful practice

Progression should reward relevant challenges, not repetitive low-risk action counts. Practice may propagate upward from a specific skill to broader competency. World knowledge, mentors, discoveries, and facilities should matter alongside practice.

## Professions belong in adventures

Professions should shape **crafting and economy**, **adventuring utility**, and **character expression**. Blacksmithing might branch into weaponsmithing and bladesmithing; an expert may recognize, repair, or prepare gear in the field. These are illustrative directions, not promised V1 mechanics.

## Pacing

The longer-term direction favors deliberate exploration and combat where preparation, resources, and choices have weight. Avoid copying inconvenience or rigid MMO roles merely to imitate an older game.

## Still unresolved

Exact classes and profession roster; progression formulas; mastery gates; dedication changes; skill-check mechanics; equipment restrictions; the number of simultaneous commitments.
